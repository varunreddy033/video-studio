## Three formats

**A. "I woke up and..." (first person, 30-45 s, comedy / romance with a twist)** - craftee
1. Impossible situation in one line: *I woke up on an island where...* / *I woke up and everyone was gone except...*
2. The rule or the problem in one line (a law, a condition).
3. Attempts or days, one image each: *On day one... On day two... On day three...* Physical comedy.
4. Clues the viewer can notice: small kind things keep happening (food left outside, a hut appears, a tally on a
   tree, a note in her handwriting).
5. A self-deprecating line: *I'm the slowest boy in my whole school.*
6. Payoff day: *On day 30, I woke up surrounded...*
7. Twist that explains every clue: *Because there's a second island law...* The last line is the punchline. Sometimes
   it mirrors the first line (opening *I said hi every morning* -> ending *she said hi*).

**B. "In this world, [deadly rule]" (third person or first, 60 s - 2:00, game / survival / fantasy)** - robloxstories
1. Rule with a deadline and a cost: *In this world, you have 10 seconds to ... or else ...*
2. Why the world does it + who watches (a crowd, a town, a school).
3. Named hero + a small named cast (short common first names). Explain the game rules in plain steps.
4. Escalation with counts: *now there were two seekers... five... nine*; a countdown: *Three seconds, two, one.*
5. Plant one tiny detail early that the twist uses (a stripe that looks different, a mask, a missing shadow).
6. Twist in the last 10-15 %: hidden in plain sight / the villain is family / the rule meant the opposite.
7. End on the reveal, or a choice question for the comments: *Who would you save? Yourself or Noah?*
   First person variant: rapid *Suddenly...* beats and 3-5 short quoted lines of dialogue.

**C. "In this town, when [X], [Y] happens" (rule horror-lite, ~60 s, third person)** - btroll
1. The rule + how people protect themselves, with a list of three for rhythm: *not kids, not grandmas, not even the
   mailman.*
2. A newcomer who doesn't believe it (*Thirteen-year-old Arlo had just moved there.*), a warning in quotes from an
   old neighbour.
3. He breaks the rule on purpose.
4. The chase in very short sentences (2-6 words): *He ran. The footsteps ran too.* One sound word: *Click!*
5. Twist: the "monster" was protecting him; something bigger is the real danger. End on that image.

(polystories is a different format: an escalating number on screen (*IQ 112 -> 1,677,721,600*) with one italic line
per beat and almost no narration. Our blocky3d renderer will get that as a HUD later; not a narrated script.)

## Sentence rules (all formats)

- The first sentence is the hook and the whole premise, <= 15 words. No intro, no "today", no channel name.
- One sentence = one picture. If it can't be filmed (an opinion, a summary), cut it or turn it into an action.
  Every sentence gets its own `[shot:]` (or a `[sign:]` for a law, note, scoreboard or phone message).
- Median 6-11 words; action and chase beats drop to 2-5 words; the rule explanation may run 15-20.
- Concrete nouns the picture can show: cliff, tree, cave, umbrella, fountain, cafeteria, shopping carts.
- Counters and repetition carry the pace: day 1/2/3, how many are left, countdowns, "Then... Then...".
- Connectors that keep momentum: *But / Then / Suddenly / Meanwhile / Because / So / That's when*.
- Dialogue is rare and short (1-5 lines), always in quotes, said by a warning figure or at the twist.
- Stakes are personal and simple: love, embarrassment, family, survival. Kids / teens, school, home, town.
- No ending CTA ("subscribe", "follow for part 2"). The last line is the reveal, the punchline or a question.
- Keep it PG: danger and threat yes, gore no. No real people, brands, games or logos (never say "Roblox").
